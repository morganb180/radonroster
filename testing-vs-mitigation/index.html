What does radon testing do?

A radon test measures your home's radon level in pCi/L. EPA's action level is 4 pCi/L.

Short-term tests run about 2 to 7 days; long-term tests run more than 90 days and better reflect the year-round average. Real estate deals usually use short-term tests. PA DEP's Radon in the Home page says that if a result is above 4 pCi/L you should consider a second test. If the result is 4-8 pCi/L, you can do a long-term (365-day) test or another short-term test, and if it is still 4 pCi/L or higher, take corrective action.

Step by step, with kit placement, home-sale rules and costs: radon testing in Pennsylvania.

What does radon mitigation do?

Mitigation lowers the radon level after a test confirms it is 4 pCi/L or higher.

Mitigation means installing a radon reduction system, usually active soil depressurization: a pipe and fan pull soil gas from under the foundation and vent it above the roof. A follow-up test should confirm the system works. See EPA's Consumer's Guide to Radon Reduction.

Who can test for or fix radon in Pennsylvania?

Testers, mitigators and labs serving the public must hold PA DEP certification (25 Pa. Code Ch. 240). They must show a current DEP photo ID on request. Every listing on this site comes from DEP's certified lists. You can also test your own home with a kit analyzed by a DEP-certified lab; if you have never tested, see free radon test kits in Pennsylvania.
