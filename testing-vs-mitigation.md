# Radon testing vs mitigation

## What does radon testing do?

A radon test measures your home's radon level in pCi/L. EPA's action level is 4 pCi/L.

Short-term tests run about 2 to 7 days; long-term tests run more than 90 days and better reflect the year-round average. Real estate deals usually use short-term tests. PA DEP's [Radon in the Home](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/programs-and-services/radiation-protection/radon-division/radon-in-the-home) page says that if a result is above 4 pCi/L you should consider a second test. If the result is 4-8 pCi/L, you can do a long-term (365-day) test or another short-term test, and if it is still 4 pCi/L or higher, take corrective action.

## What does radon mitigation do?

Mitigation lowers the radon level after a test confirms it is 4 pCi/L or higher.

Mitigation means installing a radon reduction system, usually active soil depressurization: a pipe and fan pull soil gas from under the foundation and vent it above the roof. A follow-up test should confirm the system works. See EPA's [Consumer's Guide to Radon Reduction](https://www.epa.gov/radon/consumers-guide-radon-reduction-how-fix-your-home).

## Who can test for or fix radon in Pennsylvania?

Testers, mitigators and labs serving the public must hold PA DEP certification ([25 Pa. Code Ch. 240](https://files.dep.state.pa.us/RadiationProtection/BureauOfRadiationProtection/BRPPortalFiles/Chapter_240_2015-04-23.pdf)). They must show a current DEP photo ID on request. Every listing on this site comes from DEP's certified lists. You can also test your own home with a kit analyzed by a DEP-certified lab; if you have never tested, see [free radon test kits in Pennsylvania](https://radonroster.com/free-radon-test-kit-pennsylvania/).
