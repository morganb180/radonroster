# Free radon test kits in Pennsylvania

Last checked 2026-10-28 against the PA DEP, American Lung Association and Delaware County pages linked below. Free-kit programs have limited supply and change, so confirm with the program before you count on one. Radon Roster does not sell or distribute kits.
A radon test is the only way to know your home's level. Pennsylvania has several free-kit sources and a low-cost store option. PA DEP says an easy home test kit can be bought at hardware or home improvement stores for about $20 to $30 ([PA DEP: Radon in the Home](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/programs-and-services/radiation-protection/radon-division/radon-in-the-home)).

## Where can I get a free radon test kit in Pennsylvania?

Free kits in Pennsylvania come from the American Lung Association with PA DEP (while supplies last), DEP's newborn program, DEP's free confirmation and post-mitigation kits, and some county programs. Details and limits for each are below.

1. **American Lung Association in Pennsylvania (with PA DEP).** PA DEP's January 2, 2024 release says DEP and the American Lung Association are providing free kits to Pennsylvanians who have not yet tested, while supplies last, funded by DEP and the EPA State Indoor Radon Grant Program ([PA DEP news release](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/newsroom/dep-highlights-national-radon-action-month-provides-free-radon-test-kits)). Request one at [lungradonkits.org/pa](https://www.lungradonkits.org/pa/). That page says kits are limited to one per family, allows 2 to 4 weeks for delivery, and says lab analysis is included at no cost. We could not confirm current stock.
2. **PA DEP newborn testing program.** Through an EPA-funded program, hospitals give parents of newborns an information packet that includes a coupon for a free radon test kit from DEP ([PA DEP](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/programs-and-services/radiation-protection/radon-division/radon-in-the-home)).
3. **PA DEP free confirmation kit.** DEP says Pennsylvanians who tested a home or other building and found screening levels "greater than 100 picocuries per liter" may receive a free short-term confirmation kit from its Bureau of Radiation Protection. Call 800-237-2366 or 717-783-3594 and ask what applies to you ([PA DEP](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/programs-and-services/radiation-protection/radon-division/radon-in-the-home)).
4. **Free long-term kit after mitigation.** If an active (fan-powered) radon system was installed in your home within the last year, DEP may send a free long-term alpha-track test kit. Mail proof of installation and a copy of your post-mitigation result below 4 pCi/L to Department of Environmental Protection, Bureau of Radiation Protection, P.O. Box 8469, Harrisburg, PA 17105-8469 (or fax 717-783-8965). DEP asks you to allow 6 to 8 weeks ([PA DEP](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/programs-and-services/radiation-protection/radon-division/radon-in-the-home)).
5. **Local programs.** Delaware County Health Department announced on April 6, 2026 that it was offering free radon test kits through its Test and Go public health kiosks. Each kit had a testing canister, instructions and a prepaid label for lab analysis ([Delaware County release (PDF)](https://www.delcopa.gov/sites/default/files/media/DCHD-Offers-Radon-Test-Kits_4-6-2026.pdf)). Call the county before you go to confirm kits are in stock. We have not verified programs in other counties; your county health department is the place to ask.

## How much does a radon test kit cost?

Store kits run about $20 to $30 per PA DEP. Kits can also be bought by mail from DEP-certified laboratories. DEP's fact sheet notes that most labs charge one flat price that includes the device and the analysis, but some kits are cheaper up front with a lab fee due later, so read the package. See the [list of DEP-certified firms by county](https://radonroster.com/pa/counties/), which includes laboratories.

## How do I use a radon test kit?

* Test in the lowest lived-in area of your home. PA DEP says radon is often highest in the basement, but ground floors can be high too, even in homes without basements. DEP says the best time to test is the colder months.
* PA DEP describes a short-term test as 2 to 7 days. Follow the directions enclosed with the kit, including keeping windows and doors closed if it says so.
* Mail the kit to the lab right away and note your result in pCi/L (picocuries per liter).

## What does my radon test result mean?

* **Below 4 pCi/L:** below EPA's action level of 4 pCi/L. DEP says to test again every five years, and after renovations or additions ([PA DEP](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/programs-and-services/radiation-protection/radon-division/radon-in-the-home); see [what is radon](https://radonroster.com/what-is-radon/) for EPA's health information).
* **4 pCi/L or higher:** do a second test to confirm. If the first result is 4 to 8 pCi/L, DEP says you can do a long-term (365-day) test or another short-term test. If it is still 4 or higher, take action ([PA DEP](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/programs-and-services/radiation-protection/radon-division/radon-in-the-home)).
* **Confirmed high:** read [radon mitigation in Pennsylvania](https://radonroster.com/radon-mitigation-pennsylvania/) and the [mitigation cost guide](https://radonroster.com/radon-mitigation-cost/), then [request quotes](https://radonroster.com/quotes/) from DEP-certified mitigators.

## When should I retest my home for radon?

PA DEP's fact sheet says to test once every five years if mitigation is not needed, and again after renovations or additions. After mitigation, DEP says to test every two years, preferably in the basement during the heating season.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are free radon test kits as accurate as store-bought kits?**

Price is not the main thing to check. PA DEP says test kits are sold at hardware and home improvement stores and by Pennsylvania-certified radon laboratories, and its fact sheet advises reading the package to see what the price covers, including the lab analysis fee. Follow the instructions that come with the kit.

**When is the best time to test?**

PA DEP says the best time to test is during the colder months, when homes are closed up and radon is most likely to build up. Use the kit in the lowest lived-in area of your home, as the American Lung Association's kit instructions advise.

**What do I do if my result is 4 pCi/L or higher?**

EPA's action level is 4 pCi/L. PA DEP advises a second test to confirm. If the first result is 4 to 8 pCi/L, you can do a long-term (365-day) test or another short-term test. If the result is still 4 pCi/L or higher, hire a DEP-certified mitigator.

**Do I need a certified tester instead of a kit?**

PA DEP's fact sheet says to hire a DEP-certified tester when buying or selling a home, or if you are uncomfortable placing and retrieving a test device. A kit is a simple option for checking your own home, so look up certified testers by county on Radon Roster.
