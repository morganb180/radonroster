How much does each radon mitigation method cost? (EPA, 2010)

In EPA's 2010 guide, subslab suction (the most common method) cost $800 to $2,500 to install, and block-wall suction cost $1,500 to $3,000. PA DEP's current typical range for a system is $800 to $1,200.

These figures come from EPA's January 2010 Consumer's Guide to Radon Reduction (archived PDF). They are older than the DEP figure above, are not Pennsylvania prices, and are not quotes. Current prices may be higher. EPA's current guide no longer lists dollar amounts (EPA).

What other radon mitigation costs should I expect?

  • Fan replacement: EPA's 2010 guide put it at about $200 to $350 including parts and labor. EPA says fans may last five years or more, and manufacturer warranties tend not to exceed five years.
  • Operating cost: $50 to $200 a year for a typical subslab system per the same EPA table, mostly fan electricity and conditioned air that escapes.
  • Follow-up testing: PA DEP says the installer should test after the system has run at least 24 hours, and you should retest every two years. A DIY kit costs about $20 to $30; see free and low-cost test kits.
  • Optional extras: EPA says options such as an extended warranty, service plan or improved appearance can add to the up-front price.

What affects the price of radon mitigation?

  • Foundation type (basement, slab, crawlspace or a mix)
  • How easily soil gas moves under the slab, and how many suction points are needed
  • Pipe routing (interior vs exterior) and fan location
  • Sealing work, sump covers, and crawlspace membranes
  • Size and design of the home and the mitigation method needed (EPA)

Ask each contractor for their DEP certification number. It's shown on every listing here.

How do I compare radon mitigation quotes?

EPA advises weighing what you get for the money: a cheaper system may cost more to operate and maintain, and the quality of materials affects how long it lasts. Ask each contractor whether the quote includes:

  • Proof of DEP certification (shown as cert IDs on every listing here)
  • Liability insurance and any required local licenses
  • Diagnostic testing before design and installation
  • A warning device that signals if the system stops working
  • Testing after installation
  • A guarantee to reach 4 pCi/L or below, and for how long
  • The total cost including taxes and permit fees, deposit and payment schedule

Source: EPA Consumer's Guide to Radon Reduction.

Frequently asked questions

How much does radon mitigation cost in Pennsylvania?

PA DEP says a typical radon system costs $800 to $1,200. National cost guides report a wider range, from $395 to $2,453, so get written quotes from several DEP-certified mitigators.

What makes one radon system cost more than another?

EPA says cost depends on the size and design of the home, the foundation type and the mitigation method needed. Pipe routing, sealing work and crawlspace membranes also affect price.

What are the ongoing costs of a radon system?

EPA's 2010 guide estimated yearly fan electricity and heating or cooling loss at $50 to $200 for a typical subslab system, and $200 to $350 to replace a fan. Those are older figures, so prices today may be higher. DEP recommends retesting every two years.

How much does a radon test cost?

PA DEP says do-it-yourself kits cost about $20 to $30 at hardware stores. A professional test is reported at $150 to $800 by one national cost guide. Free kits also exist, see our free radon test kit guide.
