Radon testing in Pennsylvania

Last checked 2026-10-30 against the PA DEP, EPA and Pennsylvania regulation sources linked on this page. General information, not legal or medical advice. Radon Roster does not sell test kits or testing services.

Short answer: test every Pennsylvania home. PA DEP says about 40% of Pennsylvania homes have radon above EPA's action level of 4 pCi/L, and that testing is the only way to know your level (PA DEP, 2026). You can use a home test kit, which DEP says costs about $20 to $30 at hardware stores, or hire a DEP-certified tester, which DEP advises when you buy or sell a home (DEP fact sheet). If your result is 4 pCi/L or higher, confirm it with a second test, then get free quotes from DEP-certified mitigators.

Who should test for radon in Pennsylvania?

Everyone. PA DEP recommends that all homes, schools and public and private buildings be tested, and EPA and the Surgeon General recommend testing all homes below the third floor.

  • Pennsylvania runs high. DEP says the state has one of the most serious radon problems in the United States (PA DEP), and that while problems are more common in some regions, any home can have high levels (PA DEP, 2026).
  • The test records agree. In DEP's public Radon Test Results dataset, 37% of 2,644,033 basement tests statewide were at or above 4 pCi/L (our county table, computed from PA Open Data). These are tests reported to DEP, not a random survey.
  • Buyers and sellers. EPA recommends testing before you put a home on the market, and knowing the radon level of any home you consider buying (EPA Home Buyer's and Seller's Guide, 2024).
  • Homes with a radon system. DEP says even mitigated homes should test every two years (PA DEP, 2026).

How do I test my home for radon?

Buy a kit analyzed by a DEP-certified lab, place it in the lowest lived-in level of your home, keep windows and doors closed, then mail it to the lab as soon as the test ends. Or hire a DEP-certified tester to do it.

  1. Pick the time. DEP says the best time to test is the colder months, when homes are closed up and radon builds up (PA DEP, 2026). EPA says not to run short tests of less than four days during severe storms or high winds (EPA).
  2. Pick the spot. Use the lowest lived-in level of the home (DEP fact sheet). DEP notes radon is often highest in the basement, but ground floors can be high too, even in homes without basements (PA DEP). EPA says to keep the device at least 20 inches above the floor, away from drafts, high heat, high humidity and exterior walls, and not in a kitchen, bathroom, laundry room, closet, hallway or crawl space (EPA).
  3. Close the house. For short-term tests under four days, EPA says to close windows and outside doors at least 12 hours before the test starts and keep them closed, except for normal entry and exit, until it ends (EPA).
  4. Run it long enough. EPA and PA DEP both say every radon test should run at least 48 hours; some devices, such as a 7-day charcoal canister, need longer (PA DEP Home Buyer's and Seller's Guide).
  5. Mail it right away. Record the start and stop times, reseal the kit and send it to the lab on the package. EPA says results usually come back within a few days or weeks (EPA).

Any store or website can sell a kit, but DEP says the lab that analyzes it must be certified by DEP. DEP adds that kits from certified labs are held to the same quality standards as the devices certified testers use (DEP fact sheet). Certified labs are listed in the county directory.

Start with a short-term test, as PA DEP recommends. Use a long-term test to confirm a result between 4 and 10 pCi/L, because it shows your year-round average.

Should I use a DIY kit or a DEP-certified tester?

A kit is fine for checking your own home. PA DEP advises hiring a DEP-certified tester when you are buying or selling a home, or if you are not comfortable placing and retrieving the device yourself.

Pennsylvania law requires anyone who performs radon testing, mitigation or lab analysis for the public to be certified by DEP and to show a current DEP photo ID card on request (25 Pa. Code Ch. 240, § 240.302). EPA says a qualified tester can recommend a testing approach, explain how to keep test conditions, analyze the data and give you an independent result (EPA). Find one in the directory of DEP-certified firms by county or the A-Z list of certified firms.

What do my radon test results mean?

EPA's action level is 4 pCi/L: fix the home at 4 or higher, and consider fixing between 2 and 4. Confirm a high short-term result with a second test first.

How does radon testing work when buying or selling a home in PA?

Hire a DEP-certified tester. Pennsylvania rules make real estate tests tamper-resistant, and the test goes in the lowest level of the home suitable for living space.

  • Anti-tampering rules. For real estate tests, certified testers must secure devices against movement, have the buyer, seller, occupant, agent or other person in control of the property sign a Conditions for Short-Term Radon Testing Agreement, and post a "Radon Testing in Progress" notice at every entry (25 Pa. Code § 240.309).
  • Where the test goes. In a sale, DEP says the test is done in the lowest level suitable for occupancy, meaning the lowest finished level, or one that needs no major structural renovation to use, usually a basement or slab-on-grade (DEP fact sheet).
  • Three short-term options. PA DEP and EPA accept two short-term tests taken at the same time, two taken back to back, or a continuous monitor, each for at least 48 hours. Fix the home if the average is 4 pCi/L or more (PA DEP guide; EPA).
  • Using the seller's test. Before accepting an earlier result, DEP says to find out the result, who ran the test, where in the home it was placed, and whether the house or its heating and cooling system has changed since (PA DEP guide).
  • Disclosure. DEP's guide says Pennsylvania law requires sellers to disclose the results of any known radon testing. See PA real estate radon disclosure basics.

How much does radon testing cost in Pennsylvania?

A home test kit costs about $20 to $30, according to PA DEP. PA DEP does not publish a price for a professional test; one national cost guide reports $150 to $800, so prices vary and you should ask for a written price.

Where can I get a free or low-cost radon test kit in PA?

The American Lung Association and PA DEP offer free kits to Pennsylvanians who have not tested yet, while supplies last. DEP also has free kits for new parents, very high screening results and recently mitigated homes.

  • American Lung Association with PA DEP: request one at lungradonkits.org/pa. The page says kits are limited, one per family, take two to four weeks to arrive, and include the lab result at no cost.
  • Newborn program: hospitals give parents of newborns a coupon for a free DEP kit (PA DEP).
  • Confirmation kit: free short-term kit if a screening test found more than 100 pCi/L (call 800-237-2366 or 717-783-3594).
  • After mitigation: a free long-term kit if an active system was installed in the last year; DEP lists what to send and asks you to allow 6 to 8 weeks.

How do I check a radon tester's DEP certification?

Ask to see the tester's current DEP photo ID card and check the name against PA DEP's Radon Services Directory, which DEP updates monthly. You can also call the PA Radon Hotline at 800-237-2366.

  • DEP publishes certified testers, mitigators and labs by name and by county; the current lists are dated October 1, 2026 (PA DEP Radon Services Directory).
  • DEP-listed testing-firm employees have ID numbers in the 8000 series, and mitigation-firm employees in the 7000 series (PA DEP).
  • DEP says to check for a current DEP ID card and/or certification certificate, because certification status can change (PA DEP).

What should I do if my radon level is high?

Confirm it with a second test, then hire a DEP-certified mitigator. PA DEP says a typical radon reduction system costs $800 to $1,200.

  • PA DEP's 2026 figure for a permanent system is $800 to $1,200 (PA DEP, 2026). See the radon mitigation cost guide for EPA's cost by method, or watch Radon mitigation cost in Pennsylvania (Radon Roster on YouTube).
  • DEP says installation should take less than a day, and the installer should run a follow-up test after the system has run at least 24 hours (PA DEP). Read how radon mitigation works in Pennsylvania.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a radon test cost in Pennsylvania?

PA DEP says a home test kit costs about $20 to $30 at hardware and home improvement stores, and some kits charge the lab fee separately. PA DEP does not publish a price for a professional test. One national cost guide (HomeAdvisor) reports $150 to $800, so ask DEP-certified testers for a written price. Free kits are sometimes available in Pennsylvania.

How long does a radon test take?

PA DEP says short-term kits usually take two to four days, and its Radon in the Home page describes short-term tests as 2 to 7 days. EPA says every radon test should run at least 48 hours. A long-term test runs more than 90 days; PA DEP suggests at least 91 days and preferably a full year.

Should I do a short-term or long-term radon test?

PA DEP recommends that homeowners test first with a short-term device. If the result is between 4 and 10 pCi/L, DEP says to consider a long-term test, because the closer a short-term result is to 4 pCi/L the harder it is to tell whether the year-round average is above or below it.

Do I need a DEP-certified tester when buying or selling a home in Pennsylvania?

PA DEP advises hiring a DEP-certified tester when buying or selling a home, or if you are uncomfortable placing and retrieving a test device yourself. Pennsylvania rules require certified testers to use anti-tampering procedures on real estate tests, including a signed non-interference agreement.

What radon level should I fix?

EPA recommends fixing a home at 4 pCi/L or more and considering a fix between 2 and 4 pCi/L, because there is no known safe level of exposure. PA DEP advises confirming a high result with a second test before you hire a DEP-certified mitigator.

How often should I retest for radon?

PA DEP says to retest every five years if mitigation was not needed, and after renovations or additions. If your home has a mitigation system, DEP recommends testing every two years.
