Radon mitigation in Pennsylvania

Radon mitigation (also called radon reduction or remediation) lowers the radon level in a home after a test finds a problem. In Pennsylvania the work must be done by a DEP-certified mitigator. This page is a short overview. Prices are on the cost guide.

When you need mitigation

EPA recommends fixing a home with a radon level of 4 pCi/L or higher. PA DEP says to confirm a high result with a second test first. If the first result is 4 to 8 pCi/L, a long-term (365-day) test or another short-term test works. If the result is still 4 or higher, take corrective action (PA DEP: Radon in the Home). Not tested yet? See free and low-cost test kits.

How a typical system works

The most common method is active soil depressurization. A contractor drills a suction point through the basement slab (or covers a crawlspace with a plastic membrane), connects PVC pipe to an inline fan, and vents the soil gas above the roofline. Major cracks and openings are sealed so the fan works better. Other methods exist for specific foundations, such as drain-tile or block-wall suction (EPA: Consumer's Guide to Radon Reduction).

What to expect

  1. The contractor inspects your home and reviews your test results before quoting.
  2. Installation is usually done in less than a day, per PA DEP.
  3. After the system has run for at least 24 hours, the installer runs a follow-up test to confirm radon dropped.
  4. The fan should run all the time. EPA says it should never be turned off, and a U-tube manometer on the pipe lets you check it.
  5. Retest every two years, preferably in the basement during the heating season, per PA DEP.

Hiring a mitigator in Pennsylvania

  • Ask for the contractor's DEP certification ID and match it to the listing. IDs are shown on every firm page.
  • Get written quotes from more than one firm. EPA suggests comparing diagnostic testing, a warning device for the fan, post-installation testing, liability insurance and any guarantee to reach 4 pCi/L or below.
  • Read the contract: total cost including taxes and permit fees, deposit, timeline, and the guarantee terms (EPA).

Frequently asked questions

How long does radon mitigation take?

PA DEP says installing a radon reduction system should take less than a day. After the system has run for at least 24 hours, the installer should do a follow-up test to confirm radon levels dropped.

How do I know the system is working?

Most systems include a U-tube manometer on the pipe. PA DEP says uneven fluid levels mean the fan is running. If the levels are even, the fan may be off or failing, so call your installer. DEP also recommends retesting every two years.

Does Pennsylvania require radon contractors to be certified?

Yes. Anyone who tests, mitigates or runs a lab for the public in Pennsylvania must be DEP-certified. Every listing on Radon Roster shows its DEP certification IDs so you can ask for them.

How much does a radon system cost?

PA DEP's typical range is $800 to $1,200, but prices vary with the home. See the radon mitigation cost guide for the ranges and what drives them.
