How to find a radon expert in Pittsburgh, PA

In Pittsburgh, 157,738 basement radon tests in PA DEP's records give a median of 2.6 pCi/L, and 31% were at or above 4 pCi/L. That's below Allegheny County as a whole (34%) and below the statewide share (37%). The median Pittsburgh result (2.6 pCi/L) is below the 4 pCi/L line; it is still in the 2-4 pCi/L range where EPA says to consider fixing. The Allegheny County median is 2.7 pCi/L.

Questions to ask a mitigator before you sign

  • Do you carry liability insurance, and will you put the full scope in writing?
  • What is your PA DEP certification number, and will you show your DEP photo ID? (Certified people must show it on request under 25 Pa. Code Chapter 240.)
  • Will you do diagnostic (sub-slab suction) testing before choosing fan size and suction points?
  • Where will the pipe run (inside or outside), and where will the fan and exhaust go?
  • What post-mitigation level do you guarantee, and what happens if the retest is high?
  • Who does the follow-up test, and is it an independent certified tester?
  • What warranty covers the fan and the work, and is it transferable if I sell?

What a typical Pennsylvania radon system install involves

Most systems use active soil depressurization (EPA Consumer's Guide to Radon Reduction). The installer drills a suction point through the basement slab (or seals a crawlspace with a membrane), runs PVC pipe to an inline fan, and vents the exhaust above the roofline. Then they seal major openings and add a pressure gauge. A follow-up test should confirm the level dropped. PA DEP describes the confirm-then-mitigate steps on PA DEP: Radon in the Home.

Buying or selling in Pittsburgh? Radon timing tips

  • Pennsylvania sellers must give buyers a property disclosure statement before an agreement of sale is signed. The Real Estate Commission's minimum form asks whether the seller knows of hazardous substances "including... radon" and whether the property has been tested (49 Pa. Code § 35.335a; 68 Pa.C.S. Chapter 73).
  • Sellers aren't required to test, but must not hide known defects (68 Pa.C.S. § 7308).
  • Real estate radon tests by certified testers follow DEP anti-tampering rules, including a signed non-interference agreement (25 Pa. Code Chapter 240). Book the test early in the inspection contingency window, because short-term tests run at least 2 days plus lab time.
  • If a result is high near closing, ask certified mitigators for written quotes. Buyers and sellers often negotiate who pays for a system.

How to verify a Pittsburgh radon contractor's PA DEP certification

  1. Check the name against DEP's current certified mitigation and testing lists in the PA DEP Radon Services Directory. The lists are updated periodically, and ours were retrieved 2026-10-11.
  2. Ask for the DEP certification number and photo ID. Pennsylvania requires anyone doing radon testing, mitigation or lab work for the public to be DEP-certified (25 Pa. Code Chapter 240).
  3. Still unsure? Call the PA DEP Radon Hotline at 800-237-2366.

Frequently asked questions

How much does radon mitigation cost in Pittsburgh?

We don't have Pittsburgh-specific price data. A national cost guide puts typical installs at $786-$1,274 (average about $1,028). Get written quotes from certified local mitigators.

Do I need a radon test when buying a home in Pennsylvania?

It isn't required by state law. The seller's disclosure form asks about known radon and past testing, and many buyers order a test during the inspection period. Real estate tests by certified testers follow DEP anti-tampering rules.

How long does a radon test take?

Short-term tests run 2 to 7 days. Long-term tests run more than 90 days. PA DEP suggests confirming a high short-term result before deciding on mitigation.

How do I find a certified radon expert in Pittsburgh, PA?

Use a firm on PA DEP's certified lists. Radon Roster lists 28 DEP-certified firms based in Pittsburgh and 16 more in Allegheny County. Confirm any name in the PA DEP Radon Services Directory or call 800-237-2366.

Is radon common in Pittsburgh?

In PA DEP's test records, 31% of 157,738 basement tests with a Pittsburgh address were at or above 4 pCi/L (median 2.6 pCi/L). Results vary house to house, so only a test tells you your level.
