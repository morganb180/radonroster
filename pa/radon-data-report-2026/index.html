Which Pennsylvania counties test highest for radon?

Fulton, Juniata and Perry counties had the highest share of basement radon tests at or above EPA's 4 pCi/L action level in PA DEP's public test records. Statewide, 37% of 2,644,033 basement tests crossed that line.

Press summary

Quotable findings (Radon Roster analysis of PA DEP radon test data, retrieved 2026-10-11):

  1. 37% of Pennsylvania basement radon tests reported to DEP (985,388 of 2,644,033) were at or above EPA's 4 pCi/L action level.
  2. Fulton County had the highest share: 70% of 707 basement tests at or above 4 pCi/L, nearly 6 times the rate in Philadelphia (12%), the lowest.
  3. In 22 of Pennsylvania's 67 counties, at least half of all basement radon tests were at or above 4 pCi/L.
  4. Even in Philadelphia, the lowest-share county, 18,896 basement tests were at or above 4 pCi/L.
  5. 3 of the 10 counties with the highest shares (Fulton, Juniata and Jefferson) have no DEP-certified radon firm with a business address in the county.

What do these radon results mean for a homeowner?

County shares describe tests, not any one house. Homes next door to each other can test very differently, and low-share counties still produce many high results. EPA recommends fixing a home at 4 pCi/L or more and considering it between 2 and 4 pCi/L (EPA). The only way to know is to test. Methodology

  • Data: PA DEP, Radon Test Results, September 1986 - Current (PA Open Data Portal, public domain), county-level aggregates retrieved 2026-10-11.
  • Measure: for each county, the number of basement test results at or above 4.0 pCi/L divided by all basement test results. First-floor shares are computed the same way.
  • Statewide share: all 985,388 basement results at or above 4 pCi/L divided by all 2,644,033 basement results across the 67 counties (test-weighted, not an average of county percentages). First floor: 78,826 of 396,546 (20%).
  • Ranking: by unrounded share, highest first; percentages are shown rounded to whole numbers. Medians are DEP basement results as aggregated at retrieval.
  • Certified firms: firms with a business address in the county on PA DEP certification lists dated 10/01/2026. Firms based elsewhere may also serve a county.
  • Limits: these are tests reported to DEP by certified testers and labs, not a random sample of homes. Repeat, real estate and post-mitigation tests are included, and counties with few tests can swing more. Basement readings usually run higher than living-floor readings.
