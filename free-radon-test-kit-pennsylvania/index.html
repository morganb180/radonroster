Free radon test kit in Pennsylvania: how to get one

If you live in Pennsylvania and have never tested your home for radon, you can order a free test kit from the American Lung Association and PA DEP, with return postage and lab results included. A store kit costs about $20 to $30, per PA DEP, so that is what you save. Here is who qualifies, the other free options, what to do if kits run out, and what to do if your number comes back high.

How do I get a free radon test kit in Pennsylvania?

Order it online from the American Lung Association's Pennsylvania page. The American Lung Association and PA DEP provide kits to Pennsylvania residents who have not yet tested their homes, one kit per family, while the limited supply lasts (ALA).

  1. Check you qualify. You live in Pennsylvania and have not tested your home before.
  2. Order online. Fill in the order form. Allow two to four weeks for delivery.
  3. Run the test. Place it in the lowest lived-in area of your home and leave it out for the number of days the directions give.
  4. Mail it right away. Fill in the form, seal the kit and send it to the lab as soon as the test ends. Postage is included, and the result is free.

Order your free PA kit

PA DEP announced the program in January 2024, saying the kits were funded by DEP and the EPA State Indoor Radon Grant Program and offered while supplies last (PA DEP, Jan 2, 2024). DEP's January 2026 radon release does not mention the free kits, so the order page is the best place to check whether they are still available (PA DEP, Jan 12, 2026). The kit is a first screening test. For a home sale, PA DEP advises hiring a DEP-certified tester instead (PA DEP fact sheet (PDF)).

Are there other free radon test kits in Pennsylvania?

Yes, in a few specific cases. PA DEP has three free-kit programs for particular situations, and some counties and townships hand out kits locally while supplies last.

PA DEP special cases

  • Very high screening result. If a screening test in your home or building came back greater than 100 pCi/L, DEP's Bureau of Radiation Protection may send a free short-term confirmation kit. Call 800-237-2366 or 717-783-3594 (PA DEP).
  • After a new radon system. If an active (fan-powered) system was installed in your home within the last year, DEP may send a free long-term alpha-track kit. Mail proof of installation and a post-mitigation result below 4 pCi/L to the Department of Environmental Protection, Bureau of Radiation Protection, P.O. Box 8469, Harrisburg, PA 17105-8469, or fax it to 717-783-8965. Allow six to eight weeks (PA DEP).
  • Parents of newborns. Through an EPA-funded program, hospitals give new parents an information packet that includes a coupon for a free radon test kit from DEP (PA DEP).

County and local programs

  • Delaware County. The Delaware County Health Department offers free radon test kits at its Test and Go kiosks, which its kiosk page (updated Sept 18, 2026) lists among the free products. Each kit includes a canister, instructions and a prepaid label for lab analysis. Kiosks: SEPTA 69th Street Transportation Terminal, 6901 Market St, Upper Darby (terminal hours); Delaware County Community College Marple Campus, 901 Media Line Rd, Media (Student Resource Center hours); Keystone First Wellness and Opportunity Center, 1929 W 9th St, Chester (24/7); Delaware County Wellness Center at Yeadon, 125 Chester Ave, Yeadon (24/7); Government Center, 201 W Front St, Media (24/7). Stock can run out, so call the Wellness Line at 484-276-2100 first (Delaware County kiosks; April 2026 release (PDF)).
  • Cranberry Township, Venango County. In January 2026 the township offered free radon kits, while supplies last, at the Township Building, 3726 State Route 257, Seneca, Monday to Friday, 7 a.m. to 3 p.m. Call (814) 676-8812 to check before you go (Cranberry Township).
  • Allegheny County. The Allegheny County Health Department gave away 900 free kits in January 2025, one per household (WESA). That giveaway has ended. To ask whether kits are available now, call the department's Housing and Community Environment office at 412-350-4046.

For programs in your area, ask your county health department or the PA Radon Hotline at 800-237-2366. Several of the local giveaways above were announced in January, which is National Radon Action Month.

What if the free kits are gone?

Buy a kit. PA DEP says hardware and home improvement stores and Pennsylvania-certified radon labs sell them for about $20 to $30 (PA DEP; Jan 2026 release).

  • Read what the price covers. DEP's fact sheet says most labs charge one flat price that includes analysis, but some kits are cheaper up front and charge a lab fee later. The lab that analyzes your kit must be DEP-certified, and kits from certified labs are held to the same quality standards as the devices certified testers use (PA DEP fact sheet (PDF)).
  • Check the lab. Labs on DEP's Oct 1, 2026 list that are based outside Pennsylvania are on our out-of-state labs list. Pennsylvania labs appear on county pages, for example Accustar Labs in Harrisburg.
  • A $20 mail-order option. Kansas State University's National Radon Program Services sells a short-term (2 to 4 day) kit for $20 and a long-term kit for $30, with all costs included. Call 1-800-767-7236. It says kits ship on Mondays and Thursdays and take one to two weeks to arrive (sosradon.org). The short-term kits are analyzed by Alpha Energy Labs in Texas, whose website names it Alpha Energy Labs – Eurofins Environment Testing Radon, LLC (aelabs.com). That lab and Radonova, which analyzes the long-term kit, appear on DEP's certified lab list: see Eurofins Environment Testing Radon and Radonova. Confirm with the seller before you buy.
  • Hire a certified tester if you are buying or selling a home, or would rather not place the kit yourself (PA DEP fact sheet (PDF)). Find DEP-certified testers by county.

How do I use a radon test kit?

Put it in the lowest lived-in level of your home, leave it for the time the directions give (2 to 7 days for a short-term test), then mail it to the lab right away.

  • Where: the lowest lived-in area, as the American Lung Association's directions say. PA DEP says radon is often highest in the basement, but ground floors can be high too, even in homes without basements (PA DEP).
  • How long: PA DEP describes a short-term test as 2 to 7 days. For a long-term test, DEP refers to a 365-day test, which better reflects your year-round level (PA DEP).
  • When: DEP says the best time to test is the colder months, when the house is closed up (PA DEP, Jan 12, 2026).
  • How: follow the kit's directions, including keeping windows and doors closed if it says so. Fill in the information the kit asks for, seal the kit and mail it as soon as the test ends.

What does my radon test result mean?

Results are in picocuries per liter (pCi/L). EPA's action level is 4 pCi/L. PA DEP says about 40% of Pennsylvania homes have tested above it (PA DEP).

  • Below 4 pCi/L: DEP says to test again every five years, and after renovations or additions (PA DEP fact sheet (PDF)). See what is radon for EPA's health information.
  • 4 pCi/L or higher: do a second test to confirm. If the first result was 4 to 8 pCi/L, DEP says you can do a long-term (365-day) test or another short-term (2 to 7 day) test. If it is still 4 or higher, take action (PA DEP).
  • Above 100 pCi/L: call DEP at 800-237-2366 about a free confirmation kit (PA DEP).
  • After mitigation: DEP says to retest every two years, preferably in the basement during the heating season (PA DEP fact sheet (PDF)).

When should I retest my home for radon?

PA DEP's fact sheet says to test once every five years if mitigation is not needed, and again after renovations or additions. After mitigation, DEP says to test every two years, preferably in the basement during the heating season.

Tested high? Find a certified mitigator

If your confirmed result is 4 pCi/L or higher, a DEP-certified mitigator can install a system. PA DEP says most systems cost $800 to $1,200 (PA DEP, Jan 12, 2026). Search your ZIP code, city or county to see certified mitigators near you.

Frequently asked questions

Are free radon test kits as accurate as store-bought kits?

Price is not the main thing to check. PA DEP says test kits are sold at hardware and home improvement stores and by Pennsylvania-certified radon laboratories, and its fact sheet advises reading the package to see what the price covers, including the lab analysis fee. Follow the instructions that come with the kit.

When is the best time to test?

PA DEP says the best time to test is during the colder months, when homes are closed up and radon is most likely to build up. Use the kit in the lowest lived-in area of your home, as the American Lung Association's kit instructions advise.

What do I do if my result is 4 pCi/L or higher?

EPA's action level is 4 pCi/L. PA DEP advises a second test to confirm. If the first result is 4 to 8 pCi/L, you can do a long-term (365-day) test or another short-term test. If the result is still 4 pCi/L or higher, hire a DEP-certified mitigator.

Do I need a certified tester instead of a kit?

PA DEP's fact sheet says to hire a DEP-certified tester when buying or selling a home, or if you are uncomfortable placing and retrieving a test device. A kit is a simple option for checking your own home, so look up certified testers by county on Radon Roster.

Is the Pennsylvania radon test kit really free?

Yes, for eligible residents. The American Lung Association's Pennsylvania kit page says postage is included and "you will receive the results at no cost to you." A store kit costs about $20 to $30, according to PA DEP, so that is roughly what the free kit saves you.

Who qualifies for a free radon test kit in Pennsylvania?

The American Lung Association and PA DEP offer the kits to Pennsylvania residents who have not yet tested their homes. The order page says the number of kits is limited and allows one kit per family.

How long does the free kit take to arrive?

The American Lung Association says to allow two to four weeks for delivery. After the test, seal the kit and mail it to the lab right away; postage is included.

Can I get a second free kit to confirm a high result?

The American Lung Association kit is meant as a first screening test, one per family. PA DEP sends a free short-term confirmation kit only when a screening result is greater than 100 pCi/L (call 800-237-2366 or 717-783-3594). Otherwise, buy a second kit or hire a DEP-certified tester.
